# Arca — Brand Lore

*arca (n.) — Latin: the strongbox. the chest where Rome kept what mattered.*

---

## I. The oldest problem

For as long as people have had something worth keeping, they built a box to keep it in — and then hired someone to guard the box.

The box was never the problem. The guard was.

Every vault in history shared the same quiet flaw: a human hand held the key. You never really trusted the vault. You trusted the person standing in front of it, and you hoped they would still be honest tomorrow. Sometimes they were. Often enough, they weren't — and by the time you found out, the box was already empty.

Crypto was supposed to end this. Instead it rebuilt it, faster. New names, same room: a founder with the multisig, a "team wallet" with the supply, a manager who swears the funds are safe right up until the morning they aren't.

## II. What a vault actually is

Strip away the marble and the marketing and a vault is two things: a place that holds value, and a rule about who can open it.

For four thousand years the rule was a person. Arca changes the rule.

## III. The turn

**Arca is the vault that guards itself.**

A basket of real assets — tokenized stocks, held on-chain — sealed by code instead of a keeper. An autonomous agent can rearrange what's inside, swapping one holding for another as the market moves. But no hand, not the agent's and not ours, can reach in and take anything out. The lock has no key. The custodian is the contract.

The agent's powers are small and fixed, and they are enforced by the box, not promised by us:

- it may **rebalance** within a fixed whitelist of assets;
- it may not **withdraw** to any outside address — ever;
- it may not **pause** your redemption;
- it may not **change the rules** after deployment.

These aren't features on a roadmap. They're what the contract will and will not let anyone do, provable today, on-chain, by anyone.

## IV. From Fides to Arca

We started as **Fides** — Latin for *faith*. It was an honest name for a dishonest era: everyone in this market asks you to have faith. Faith in the team. Faith in the treasury. Faith that the manager won't touch your money.

But our whole thesis was the opposite of faith. *Don't trust — verify.* A name that means "have faith" was fighting the product every time we said it.

So we kept the discipline and dropped the word. **Fides** was the promise. **Arca** is the proof. Not "trust us, your money is safe" — but "here is the box, the door is glass, look inside."

Same engine. Same on-chain vault. A name that finally tells the truth about it.

## V. The invitation

Every fund, every exchange, every custodian has told you the same sentence for a hundred years: *trust us, your money is safe.*

Arca says the opposite.

**Don't trust us. Look inside.** Every asset, every rebalance, every price — on-chain, in order, forever. Reproduce any number yourself with a single call. The keeper can't run off with the box, because there is no keeper.

The door is glass.
